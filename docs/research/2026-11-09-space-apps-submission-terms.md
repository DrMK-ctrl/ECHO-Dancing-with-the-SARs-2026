# NASA Space Apps 2026 submission terms and ECHO's private repository

Checked: 2026-11-09 against NASA Space Apps' official 2026 site and participant terms.

## Confirmed from official sources

- The 2026 challenge runs November 14–15. The official challenge site says project submission opens November 14 at 09:00 and closes November 15 at 23:59 local time. Registration is required for participation and Global Judging eligibility.
- The participant terms, updated August 7, 2026, say submission grants NASA permission to display, copy, combine, compile, distribute, and disseminate the submitted solution.
- The terms say original content created for Space Apps must be freely available without restriction or licensed under an OSI-approved open-source license. If no specific OSI-compliant license is identified, NASA says it will distribute the original content under Apache 2.0. Posted solutions may be made available for others to download or use.
- NASA's official challenge page is [Dancing with the SARs](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/dancing-with-the-sars/); the official [participant terms](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/legal/) provide the submission and intellectual-property language.

## Official GO Team clarification — 2026-11-12

The NASA Space Apps Global Organizing Team answered a public question on November 5 in [Space Apps discussion #1827](https://github.com/nasa/spaceapps/discussions/1827). The question asked what participants may do before the event. The GO Team says participants should refrain from starting their project until the official November 14–15 event begins; working ahead can lead to disqualification. Before then, participants may select a challenge, review resources, research the topic, and form a team. The GO Team also says Global Award eligibility requires at least one NASA data source listed in the challenge's Resources, and that used data, resources, and tools must be listed in the submission.

This is stronger and more current than the older 2022 FAQ cited in the charter. The public answer is general guidance, not an individualized decision about ECHO or a ruling from the Beirut Local Lead. The current challenge Resources list was not yet available in the reviewed page as of November 12; verify it when released before claiming that ECHO's NASA data source satisfies the specific requirement.

## ECHO implication

ECHO's GitHub repository remains private under the user's explicit direction. It has not been published, relicensed, or made available to judges. Because the project has been developed specifically in preparation for this challenge, it is not safe to assume that keeping all original challenge work private will satisfy the submission terms. The terms do not clearly resolve here how pre-event preparation code, an exported source bundle, third-party assets, and the full repository should be treated in this project.

ECHO's Git history begins on November 7, before the hackathon start. The GO Team's guidance makes this a material eligibility risk: substantial pre-event project implementation may be considered working ahead and may lead to disqualification. The answer does not state a grandfathering exception for existing products or specify whether a new, separately built solution may use pre-event research while excluding pre-event code and assets. Do not represent ECHO as developed during the event or assume that disclosure alone cures the risk.

The project lead should obtain a private, project-specific interpretation from the Beirut Local Lead or GO Team before entering ECHO. The connected Slack account currently exposes only `#all-slack` and `#social`, with no Beirut event channel available, and no message has been sent. Keep the repository private and preserve its authentic history. Do not publish, submit, export, or distribute source/materials without separate explicit approval and a decision about the participant terms.

The challenge's required NASA Resources list is scheduled to be available November 13 according to the user's organizer materials; verify the actual official list then. The GO Team's answer says at least one listed NASA data source is required for Global Award eligibility, even though other data sources may supplement it.
