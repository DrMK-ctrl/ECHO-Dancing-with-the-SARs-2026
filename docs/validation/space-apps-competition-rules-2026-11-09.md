# NASA Space Apps 2026 submission and release checks

**Reviewed:** 2026-11-09
**Eligibility inquiry update:** 2026-11-12

**Status:** Official event and participant terms reconfirmed from NASA Space Apps indexed content; the registration deadlines differ, the participant's registration state is unknown, and eventual submission has a release/licensing consequence

## Verified from official NASA Space Apps indexed content

- NASA Space Apps' [2026 event page](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/) states that the event is November 14–15, 2026; project submission opens November 14 at 9:00 a.m. and closes November 15 at 11:59 p.m. local time. It says registration remains open through the hackathon close.
- The [2026 Participant Terms and Conditions](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/legal/) state that participants must register before the event start. This is an earlier deadline than the event-page registration window. Registering before the event begins satisfies both published statements; ECHO has not checked the project lead's account or registration state.
- The participant terms state that posted solutions will be available for others to download or use. Original content created for the challenge must be freely available without restriction or licensed under an OSI-compliant open-source license. If no OSI-compliant license is specified, the terms say NASA will distribute that original content under Apache 2.0.
- The terms place responsibility on participants to verify third-party material rights, identify applicable licenses in submitted documentation, and cite every source used in creating the project.

## ECHO release boundary

These terms are not authorization to publish ECHO or change repository visibility. The project lead's standing instruction keeps the repository private unless publication is explicitly authorized. Submitting a solution may result in public distribution and may require open licensing; therefore the private repository and a potential submission package must be treated as separate decisions. Do not submit, publish, or relicense project materials without the project lead's approval of the exact package and its release terms.

Before a submission package is prepared, review every included source, dataset, dependency, logo, and other third-party asset against the terms; record its attribution and license. Do not assume that a private development repository can remain the only judge-facing project location if the submission platform requires accessible materials.

## Beirut presentation requirements supplied by the project lead

The project lead relayed local organizer requirements: either a demonstration video up to 30 seconds or a presentation up to 7 slides, plus a separate two-minute judging video; the judging video should explain the approach, demonstrate the solution, identify scientific data and methods, distinguish ECHO, and discuss challenges and limitations. Supporting research, references, project information, and development progress are to be documented in the team's Slack channel.

These local requirements are recorded as organizer guidance supplied by the project lead; this review did not find an independently accessible Beirut 2026 event page confirming those details. A connected Slack workspace check on 2026-11-12 exposed only `#all-slack` and `#social`; targeted searches found no Beirut Space Apps messages or files. The connected workspace therefore did not provide independent confirmation. No presentation or video work is part of this validation.

To independently verify the local instructions, ECHO still needs an accessible Beirut 2026 organizer briefing, event-channel export, or equivalent official message. Until then, retain these as user-supplied requirements and do not generalize them to NASA-wide rules.

## Pre-event development eligibility

The current [2026 Participant Terms](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/legal/) and [2026 event page](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/) establish registration, event, and submission timing, but the reviewed text does not explicitly say whether participants may build the challenge solution before the event begins. NASA's official [2022 Participant FAQ](https://legacy.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/faq/) asked participants to start actual challenge work when that year's hackathon began. That is historical 2022 guidance, not a verified 2026 rule, and cannot settle the current requirement. The 2026 Beirut Local Event page could not be retrieved (HTTP 400 timeout), and no current Beirut pre-event development rule was independently verified.

The 2026 NASA Space Apps page for the [Santiago, Dominican Republic local event](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/local-events/santiago-dominican-republic/) currently says teams may prepare before the event, but may not begin developing the solution they intend to submit until the official start. This appears on a local-event page rather than in the participant terms, so it is evidence that event-specific guidance exists, not proof that the same rule applies in Beirut.

ECHO has substantial pre-event code, research, and data-screening work. This history is preserved in the private repository and its dated commits. Do not represent pre-event work as work performed during the 48-hour event. On 2026-11-11, a written clarification request was sent to the Space Apps contact address displayed on current 2026 event pages (`info@spaceappschallenge.org`). It asks whether a substantially pre-developed project may be submitted, what disclosure or development limits apply, and whether Beirut has a distinct local rule. No response was present in the connected inbox when checked on 2026-11-12. Eligibility and required disclosure therefore remain unresolved; preserve the history and do not imply that the inquiry itself establishes permission.
